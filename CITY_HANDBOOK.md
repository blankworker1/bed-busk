# Bed & Busk — City Handbook

Last updated: 2026-09-21

A repeatable playbook for bringing Bed & Busk to a new city, generalized from the Bosa pilot. The growth model is **city by city, year by year** — each new city is its own small campaign, not a rollout pushed from the center.

## The Core Idea

A new city doesn't join Bed & Busk as an act of charity or platform adoption for its own sake — it joins because Bed & Busk becomes **part of that city's own tourism support strategy**, backed by a local cultural or tourism body, not by Bed & Busk's own marketing budget.

This is also the funding model. Bed & Busk doesn't need a central war chest to expand — each city brings its own local partner (a cultural foundation, a tourism office, a comune) who has reason to fund or support the launch locally, because it serves *their* goals: off-season visitor draw, cultural programming, civic identity, revitalization. Growth is funded per-city, by the city, not by a central raise.

## The Partnership Model (Generalized)

The mechanism that makes this an easy "yes" for local hospitality businesses, wherever it runs:

- A participating B&B, guesthouse, or small hotel designates **exactly one room** as a "barter room"
- That room is offered through Bed & Busk in exchange for a short performance — in-kind, no cash
- Every other room continues as normal paid business — no change to core revenue
- The barter room may be restricted to **low season only**, filling capacity that would otherwise sit empty

This is the one piece that should **not** change city to city — it's what makes the ask low-risk enough for a business to say yes without a long negotiation. What changes per city is who the local partner is, what the civic "why now" story is, and the specific numbers in the objectives section.

## Onboarding Steps

1. **Find the local equivalent of Fondazione Bosa.** Every candidate city has some version of this — a cultural foundation, a tourism board, a comune's cultural office, a "welcome" organization (see Bologna Welcome, Cervia's tourism foundation, etc. — this structure is common across Italian towns specifically). Look for a body already running cultural programming, ideally one with a track record of partnering with *outside* cultural groups, not just producing everything in-house — that's the strongest signal they'll say yes to a partner they didn't invent themselves.
2. **Adapt the one-page pitch.** Use `pitch/bosa-2027-pitch.html` as the template — same five-section structure (Project, Mechanism, Responsible Body, Economic Sustainability, Objectives & Indicators), same barter-room mechanism, swap in the new city's name, partner, and locally realistic numbers.
3. **Identify whether the city has never held a comparable title.** Bosa's pitch worked partly because it was a genuine first — "the town has never had a comune-wide annual cultural title." Check this for each candidate city; it's a real, usable hook when true, and a signal to drop or reframe when it isn't.
4. **Seed the pilot cohort.** Once a local partner is on board, this is the same Phase 0 already documented in `GROWTH_STRATEGY.md` — hand-recruit the first 15–25 patrons directly, populate the city's Guide, get pitches mapped, before opening artist signups.
5. **Add the city to the platform.** One line in the `CITIES` array on the homepage (`wireframes/index.html`), plus a new city hub page built the same way `bosa.html` was.

## Relationship to the Growth Strategy

`GROWTH_STRATEGY.md` covers how Bed & Busk acquires **artists and patrons** within a city already up and running (festival-anchored acquisition, adjacent communities, SEO, SMO). This handbook covers the step before that: how a **new city** gets a local partner, a funded launch, and a reason to exist on the platform in the first place. Both run in parallel once there's more than one active city — city-partnership acquisition and artist/patron acquisition are two different motions, not one continuous funnel.
