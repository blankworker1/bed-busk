# Bed & Busk — Growth Strategy

Last updated: 2026-09-21

Target: 2,500 active artist profiles — enough to make Bed & Busk the largest platform built specifically for touring performers, ahead of busk.co's registered base of roughly 1,300–1,700 artists built up over a decade. Not the largest hospitality-exchange platform overall (Couchsurfing and Workaway operate in the millions) — the largest one built for this specific audience, which is the correct comparison since neither of those serves touring performers' particular needs.

## Phase 0 — Seed One Pilot City (Month 1–2)

Before any acquisition push, one city needs real density first: patrons, pitches, and Guide content. Artists landing in an empty directory churn immediately.

- Hand-recruit 15–25 patrons directly for the pilot city, rather than cold outreach
- Target communities already primed for gift-economy hosting: Couchsurfing "super hosts," the house-concert circuit, arts-patron types
- Populate the pilot city's Guide (pitches, permits, etiquette) before opening artist signups

## Phase 1 — Festival-Anchored Artist Acquisition (Ongoing, Seasonal)

The highest-leverage channel, proven by busk.co's own history: at Edinburgh Fringe in 2016, 80 performers signed up in a single in-person push. Touring performers self-select into festival crowds already and arrive primed to think about their next stop.

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Target events/year | 5–8 European/UK busking and street-theatre festivals (Edinburgh Fringe, Buskers Bern, Pilsen Busking Arts Fest, regional BuskFest-type events) |
| Signups per event | \~100–150, with an in-person table and a two-minute pitch |
| Annual yield | 500–1,000 artists/year if run systematically |

busk.co tried this tactic once or twice but never repeated it as a standing strategy — running it every season is the gap to exploit.

## Phase 2 — Adjacent-Community Acquisition (Ongoing)

Touring artists already active on busk.co, in busker Facebook/Reddit groups, or on Workaway/Couchsurfing as artists-in-transit are a warm audience — they already understand barter-hospitality, they're just not served by anything performance-specific. Bed & Busk doesn't compete with busk.co directly (no tips/gigs layer), so a friendly cross-referral relationship is worth pursuing rather than treating it as a rival to poach from.

## Phase 3 — Patron Growth Paced to Artist Demand

Patron supply tracks artist demand per city, not the reverse. Starting ratio: roughly one active patron per 3–5 artists per city, adjusted once real booking-frequency data exists. A directory full of empty listings is where the whole model fails to prove out.

## Phase 4 — The Referral Loop

A touring artist plans multi-city routes, which single-trip travelers don't. This creates two built-in growth mechanisms unavailable to single-destination platforms:

- A good stay in one city creates a natural reason to recommend the platform to a fellow touring artist heading to the *next* city on a shared circuit
- Guide and pitch contributions give artists a reason to stay engaged with the platform between actual bookings, not only during them

## Phase 5 — SEO: Blog & Comparison Pages

Cheap, evergreen content aimed at people already searching the category — modeled on Artaway's approach (a Journal, topic guides, and direct "X vs Y" comparison pages):

- A blog/Journal covering touring-artist and busking topics — city guides, busking-law explainers, artist and patron stories
- Direct comparison pages: "Bed & Busk vs. Couchsurfing," "vs. Workaway," "vs. busk.co" — capturing people already searching for this kind of platform
- Low cost to produce, compounds over time, and runs in parallel with the festival-acquisition channel rather than competing with it

## Phase 6 — SMO: Social Media Optimization

Presence on the channels touring artists and buskers already use to find each other and get discovered:

- Instagram and TikTok — short clips of performances, patron stays, and pitches, reshared with permission from artists' own content
- Ongoing presence in existing busker-community spaces (Facebook groups, subreddits) — the same communities as Phase 2, but sustained visibility rather than one-time recruitment
- Consistent handles and branding across platforms so a search for the platform's name surfaces the same identity everywhere

## Defining Success — Active vs. Registered

busk.co's public figures (1,387 buskers, etc.) read as *registered* profiles, not necessarily active ones — a common vanity-metric trap. Optimizing purely for signups risks hitting 2,500 registered artists while most are dormant after one look.

"2,500" should be defined as **active** profiles from the outset:

- [ ] Completed offer/expectation profile
- [ ] At least one confirmed match or review on record

This keeps the "largest platform" claim true in the way that matters, not just true on a signup counter.
